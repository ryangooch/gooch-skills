# Pattern examples and alternatives

Use these examples to recognize a habit and choose a revision strategy. Copy neither the sentence frames nor the replacements automatically.

## Contents

- Rule of three
- Negative parallelism

- Stock vocabulary

## Rule of three

Mechanical:

> **Construction and Renovation**: For cutting drywall, plywood, and other construction materials.

The third item vaguely repeats the category named by the heading.

Revised:

> **Construction and renovation:** Cut drywall and plywood with the coarse blade. Use the carbide blade for cement board.

This version groups materials by the advice that applies to them. It does more than alter the count.

Mechanical:

> The policy is ambitious, flexible, and forward-looking.

Revised:

> The policy would raise the emissions target while letting states choose how to meet it.

The revision replaces three broad compliments with testable information.

A four-item list can break a repeated three-beat cadence when all four details matter:

> He was a great hitter. He controlled the strike zone, recognized when to hit for power, worked umpires, and handled every pitch type.

Do not add a fourth detail as filler. A natural two-item list, five-item inventory, developed example, or sentence without a list may work better.

Keep a triad when reality supplies three categories:

> The study assigned participants to placebo, low-dose, and high-dose groups.

## Negative parallelism

Mechanical:

> The issue here isn’t just sourcing; it’s framing.

Direct:

> The sources are adequate, but the framing misstates what they establish.

The revision names the actual status of each issue instead of pretending to correct an unspecified belief.

Mechanical:

> This dispersal is not dissolution. Rather, it constitutes what Deleuze might describe as “becoming.”

Direct:

> Deleuze might describe this dispersal as “becoming.” The parts persist while their relations change.

Mechanical:

> Chiang prioritized the empirical consolidation of power rather than ideological purity.

Direct when the contrast is unsupported:

> Chiang used military suppression to consolidate power amid fragmented loyalties.

Direct when evidence supports both sides of the contrast:

> Chiang accepted ideological inconsistency when it helped him consolidate military control.

Keep negation when it resolves a live distinction:

> The detector reports calibrated probability, not class membership.

## Inflated copular substitutes

Inflated:

> The archive serves as the main source for the study.

Plain:

> The archive is the study’s main source.

Inflated:

> The vote marks the first defeat of the bill.

Plain:

> The vote is the bill’s first defeat.

Inflated:

> The museum boasts four galleries and features a sculpture garden.

Plain:

> The museum has four galleries and a sculpture garden.

Inflated:

> Ablation refers to the removal of a model component for testing.

Plain:

> Ablation is the removal of a model component for testing.

Keep semantic differences:

> During emergencies, the school gym functions as a shelter.

Here `functions as` describes a contingent use. Replacing it with `is` would imply a permanent identity.

## Stock vocabulary

Watch for these words and phrases, especially when several appear together:

`Additionally`; `align with`; `boasts`; `bolstered`; `crucial`; `deep dive`; `delve`; `emphasizing`; `enduring`; `enhance`; `fostering`; `garner`; `highlight` as a verb; `interplay`; `intricate` and `intricacies`; `key` as an adjective; `landscape` as an abstract noun; `meticulous` and `meticulously`; `pivotal`; `robust`; `showcase`; `tapestry` as an abstract noun; `testament`; `underscore` as a verb; `valuable`; `vibrant`.

Common repairs:

| Stock wording | Possible repair |
| --- | --- |
| Additionally | `Also`, a logical connective, or no transition |
| align with | `matches`, `agrees with`, `supports`, or a precise relation |
| bolstered | `strengthened`, `increased`, or the measured effect |
| crucial, pivotal, key, valuable | State what depends on it or what it enables |
| deep dive, delve | `examine`, `analyze`, or name the specific task |
| emphasize, highlight, underscore | `says`, `shows`, `focuses on`, or the actual evidence |
| enhance, foster | `improve`, `help create`, or the observed change |
| garner | `receive`, `earn`, `attract`, or the measured result |
| interplay | `interaction`, or describe how the factors affect one another |
| intricate | `complex`, or name the source of complexity |
| landscape | `field`, `market`, `setting`, or the concrete domain |
| meticulous | `careful`, `detailed`, or the procedure followed |
| robust | Name the perturbations, samples, or failure conditions tested |
| showcase | `show`, `present`, or state what the example establishes |
| tapestry, testament, vibrant | Replace the metaphor or praise with concrete description |

Clustered:

> Additionally, the study highlights the intricate interplay of several key factors, underscoring the model’s robust and enduring value.

Concrete:

> The study finds that soil moisture changes the model’s error most, especially during heat waves. That result held across all four test regions.

The second version supplies the mechanism and evidence that the stock words had concealed.

## Boldface

Mechanical:

> A **leveraged buyout (LBO)** uses substantial **debt financing**. **Private equity firms** invest a smaller share of their own capital, while the company’s **assets and future cash flows** secure the loans.

Revised:

> A leveraged buyout (LBO) uses substantial debt financing. A private equity firm invests a smaller share of its own capital, while the company’s assets and expected cash flows secure the loans.

Useful navigational emphasis:

> **Warning:** Disconnect power before opening the panel.

Boldface earns its place here because the warning must remain visible during scanning.

## Em dashes

Overused:

> The first trial—which enrolled 42 patients—ended early, and the second trial—which used a lower dose—did too.

Revised:

> The first trial, which enrolled 42 patients, ended early. The second trial used a lower dose and also ended early.

Routine apposition:

> One variable—soil moisture—accounted for most of the error.

Revised:

> One variable, soil moisture, accounted for most of the error.

Deliberate interruption:

> She reached for the switch—but the lights had already gone out.

The dash may remain because it creates an abrupt turn that suits the sentence.
