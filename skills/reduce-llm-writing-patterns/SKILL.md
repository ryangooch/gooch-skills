# Reduce LLM Writing Patterns

Produce direct prose whose structure follows the subject. Use the listed patterns as audit signals and allow contextual exceptions. Preserve quotations, terms of art, genuine logical contrasts, and wording the user explicitly requests.

## Draft or revise

1. Read the whole passage before changing sentences. Infer its audience, register, purpose, and existing voice.
2. Find repeated habits at both sentence and document scale. Pay more attention to clusters than isolated instances.
3. Revise the thought beneath the pattern. Do not disguise a stock construction with a synonym.
4. Preserve factual claims and qualifications. Do not add examples merely to change a list's length.
5. Read the revision for rhythm. Vary sentence length and structure without making variety conspicuous.
6. Return clean prose unless the user asks for an audit, commentary, or tracked explanation.

## Audit the six patterns

### Rule of three

Flag repeated or ornamental groups of three, especially adjective triplets, slogan-like conclusions, and lists whose items receive no development. Keep a three-part list when the subject naturally has three parts or when all three items carry useful information.

Choose among these revisions:

- Remove weak or overlapping items.
- Add a substantively necessary item when one is genuinely missing.
- Develop the most important item instead of listing peers.
- Split a broad list into prose that explains relationships.
- Vary list lengths across the piece.

Do not mechanically convert every triplet into a pair or quartet.

### Negative parallelism

Flag formulas such as “not just X, but also Y,” “not X, but Y,” “X is not merely Y,” and “X rather than Y” when they invent an unspoken misconception or create theatrical contrast.

State the affirmative claim directly. Name both alternatives only when the distinction is part of the evidence, argument, or user request. Retain concise negation when correcting an actual error or distinguishing genuinely confusable concepts.

### Inflated copular substitutes

Prefer plain verbs when the sentence only assigns identity, possession, inclusion, or definition:

- Prefer `is` or `are` to `serves as`, `stands as`, `marks`, `functions as`, `operates as`, or `represents`.
- Prefer `has`, `includes`, or a precise verb to `boasts`, `features`, `maintains`, or `offers`.
- Prefer a direct definition to `refers to` when possible.

Keep the longer verb when it adds meaning. “The clinic operates as a shelter after floods” describes a temporary use; “The clinic is a shelter” changes the claim.

### Stock vocabulary density

Scrutinize repeated or clustered use of the watch list in [references/patterns.md](references/patterns.md). Delete empty intensifiers and framing phrases. Replace remaining words with ordinary language or, better, state the concrete action, mechanism, evidence, or consequence.

Do not perform blind synonym substitution. A precise use of `robust` in statistics or `key` for a database field may be correct.

### Boldface

Use boldface for sparse navigational emphasis when it helps scanning. Avoid bolding terms throughout ordinary prose, repeating the same emphasized phrase, or turning every bullet into a labeled takeaway. Prefer headings, sentence order, paragraph breaks, and syntax to signal importance.

Respect formatting conventions and explicit user requests.

### Em dashes

Flag em dashes when commas, parentheses, colons, semicolons, or separate sentences express the relation more plainly. Avoid using several in a short passage or relying on them for routine apposition.

Keep an em dash when an abrupt break, interruption, or deliberate shift in cadence earns it. Do not replace it mechanically with a hyphen.

## Diagnose without flattening the prose

- Preserve useful emphasis, cadence, humor, and idiosyncrasy.
- Prefer concrete nouns and verbs over abstract promotional language.
- Avoid adding throat-clearing, summaries, headings, or lists that the source does not need.
- Keep technical vocabulary when it is the clearest vocabulary.
- Follow genre norms. A reference guide may need headings; a mathematical explanation may require explicit contrasts.
- Apply the same standards to any editorial explanation you write.

## Use the examples

Read [references/patterns.md](references/patterns.md) when teaching these patterns, auditing a substantial passage, or choosing among revisions. Use it as a pattern library. Avoid copying its phrasing automatically.

## Format the response

For a rewrite, provide the revised text and only the briefest necessary note. For an audit, quote short affected passages, name the pattern, explain why it weakens this passage, and offer a contextual revision. Leave harmless instances alone and report that judgment.
